Do early hotel bookings cancel more?

A city hotel cancels about 42% of its bookings. I tested whether bookings made more than 90 days ahead are the problem, and whether the gap is big enough to change the cancellation policy.

The question

The analysis is written for the hotel's revenue manager. The decision is whether to tighten cancellation rules for bookings made more than 90 days before arrival. Because a stricter rule could push guests away, I set the bar before running anything: the gap had to be at least 10 percentage points to be worth acting on.

What I did

  • Cleaned the public Hotel Booking Demand dataset (119,390 rows), removing 184 bookings with no guests, impossible prices or undefined segments.
  • Split City Hotel bookings into early (more than 90 days ahead) and late, and compared cancellation rates with a z-test for two proportions, confirmed with a chi-square test.
  • Challenged the result three ways: removing duplicate rows, repeating the test inside the largest booking channel, and checking whether the same pattern appears at a second hotel.

What I found

Early bookings cancel almost twice as often. The gap shrinks when I control for who books early, but it stays above the 10-point bar in every check.

One more signal stood out. Among early online-agency bookings, guests who made no special request cancelled 66% of the time, against 33% for guests who made at least one. That is consistent with people holding a free room as a placeholder while they keep shopping.

Recommendation

  • Shorten the free-cancellation window for online-agency bookings made more than 90 days ahead, and pilot it on selected arrival dates for 8 to 12 weeks before rolling it out.
  • Send a reconfirmation email to early bookers with no special requests, and treat them as higher risk when setting overbooking levels.
  • Handle group and offline-agent cancellations through contract terms, since lead time is not what drives them.

Limits

This is observational data from two hotels with no booking ID or cancellation date. It shows a strong association, but it cannot prove that booking early causes cancellations or predict how guests would react to a new rule. That is why the recommendation is a pilot.
